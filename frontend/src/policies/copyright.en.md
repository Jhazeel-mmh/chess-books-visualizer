# Copyright policy

Last updated: October 20, 2026.

This is a translation of the Spanish version, which prevails if they differ.

We respect copyright and ask you to do the same. This policy explains how we handle notices from rights holders, under article 114 Octies of Mexico's Federal Copyright Law and the United States copyright law (DMCA, 17 U.S.C. § 512).

## 1. How this site works

Each person uploads to their library books they declare they lawfully have, only for their own study. Libraries are private: nothing is published or shared between users. We don't review what each person uploads, but we act as soon as a rights holder tells us.

## 2. How to send a notice

Use the [notice form](/copyright), or write to {copyrightEmail}. The notice must include:

1. The name of the rights holder, or of whoever acts on their behalf, and how to reach them: email, phone and postal address.
2. The work: title, author, publisher, edition or ISBN.
3. Whatever helps find the copies. As libraries are private, you can't see what was uploaded: name the work and, if you have it, choose your copy of the file in the form. Your browser works out its fingerprint (SHA-256) and sends us only that, to find exact copies; the file never leaves your computer.
4. A statement that you believe in good faith that the use is not authorized by the rights holder, its agent, or the law.
5. A statement, under penalty of perjury, that the information is accurate and that you are the rights holder or authorized to act on their behalf.
6. Your signature; typing your full name is enough.

We don't ask for certificates of registration of the work. Whoever knowingly sends a false notice may be liable for the damage it causes.

## 3. What we do when we receive it

- We review the notice and, if it is complete, at once disable the copies of the work we find: their owner can no longer open them.
- We may block the file's fingerprint so that nobody uploads that same file again.
- We tell the owner of each copy by email: which book was disabled, on whose behalf the notice was sent and about which work, and how to answer.
- If the notice is incomplete or isn't about material on this site, we will tell you.

## 4. Counter-notice

If you think your book was disabled by mistake or misidentification, or that you have the right to use it (through a license, a contract, a limitation or exception in the law, or because the work is in the public domain), you can answer with a counter-notice from the link in the email we sent you. The counter-notice includes your name, address and phone, the reason, your statements under penalty of perjury, and your signature.

- We at once send a copy of the counter-notice to whoever sent the notice.
- Unless, within the next {waitDays} business days, they show us that they started a court or administrative action, filed a criminal complaint, or started mediation or arbitration, we enable the book again and withdraw the strike.
- A book disabled with no counter-notice is deleted after {keepDays} days.

## 5. Repeat infringement

Each notice that disables books of an account is a strike, unless a counter-notice withdraws it. At {strikes} strikes, the account is closed and its library is deleted after {keepDays} days. Strikes are counted by email address: deleting the account doesn't remove them, and that email can't sign up again if its account was closed.
